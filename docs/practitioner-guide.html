About Hilom

A small, curated roster — not a marketplace of everybody

Hilom Collective is a Philippine wellness studio. We publish courses, run live experiences, and connect people with practitioners for one-to-one work.

The practitioner side of Hilom exists because the people who do this work well are rarely the people who enjoy running a booking system. Coaches, breathwork facilitators, somatic practitioners and therapists-adjacent professionals spend their evenings chasing payment links, rewriting the same "does Thursday suit?" email, and reconciling who has paid for what.

So we built the boring half. You keep the practice — how you work, what you charge, who you take on, and what you promise. Hilom takes a percentage of what you earn through the platform and does the scheduling, the payments, the reminders, the receipts and the records.

Every practitioner on Hilom is reviewed by a person before they appear. That is slower than open signup and it is the point: a client booking an hour of intimate 1:1 work is trusting the roster as much as the profile.

Why practise here

Four things you stop doing on your own

Clients book themselves

You set your weekly hours once. Hilom projects them into real dates, subtracts your buffer, your notice period and your daily limit, and shows a client only the times you would actually say yes to. Two people cannot take the same hour.

The money arrives without asking

Payment is taken at booking. You see your fee split in full — gross, our percentage, your net — and get an email the moment a transfer leaves our account. No invoices, no reminding anyone.

Your policy is enforced, not decorative

You set the notice you need for a full refund and for a half. That is what a cancelling client is actually charged, and the wording they agree to is generated from the same numbers. What you promise and what happens cannot drift apart.

Nobody swaps personal emails

Every booking carries its own message thread. "Running five minutes late" reaches your client without either of you handing over a private address, and it stays attached to the session it is about.

Chapter One

Applying

The application is not a profile. It asks what you do, how long you have been doing it, and what kind of support you are looking for from Hilom — whether that is shaping an offering, bringing something online, or running a live experience. If you are not sure, say so; it is a real answer and choosing nothing is allowed.

You can attach a certification or affiliation document. Only Hilom sees it. It is stored privately and never appears on your profile.

Your credentials, scope of practice and the description of your approach are not on this form. You write those later, in your own dashboard, once you have been approved — so nobody drafts a public profile for an application that has not been read yet.

What happens next

One of three answers, and you will be told which:

  • Proceed — there is a strong fit. We confirm the pathway and terms.
  • Develop further — there is potential, but the offering needs more shape first.
  • Not right now — the fit or the timing is not right today.

Approved is not the same as listed. Being approved gives you the dashboard so you can set yourself up in private. Hilom publishes you to the public directory afterwards, once your profile is ready. If you are approved and clients cannot find you, this is why — not a fault.

One practical thing: sign in again after approval. Your access attaches to a fresh sign-in, so an old browser session will keep telling you that you have no dashboard.

Chapter Two

Your profile

This is the page a stranger reads before deciding to spend an hour alone with you. It carries your name and headline, your photo, your approach, your credentials, your scope of practice, the languages you work in, and whether you work online, in person or either.

Two fields do more work than they look like they do. Specialties are how clients narrow the directory. Scope of practice is the statement that keeps everyone honest — coaches, breathwork facilitators and licensed psychologists are not interchangeable, and a client should never infer clinical treatment from a booking page.

Your timezone lives here too, and it matters more than it looks: every hour you offer is stored against it.

Chapter Three

What you sell

Three kinds of thing, and most practitioners start with the first two.

  • A complimentary intro call. Free, short, and capped at one per client for ever. It is the lowest-risk way for someone to find out whether you are right for them. You can have one active at a time.
  • A single session. One paid session at a price you set.
  • A package of sessions. A block sold as one payment — six sessions, a three-month container — booked one at a time as you go.

Your cancellation policy

You set two numbers: how much notice earns a full refund, and how much earns a half. Below the second, the session is not refundable.

Those two numbers are the policy. The refund a cancelling client actually receives is computed from them, and the sentence shown on your profile, at checkout and in the client's cancel dialog is generated from the same values — so what a client is promised and what they are paid cannot disagree.

If you have written a cancellation note before, check it now. The free-text note used to be exactly that — a note. The platform applied a fixed 24-hour and 12-hour ladder regardless of what it said. That is fixed, and every existing service was left on 24/12 rather than having a policy guessed for it. If your note says something else, set the numbers to match.

Asking something before you meet

Each service can carry a short intake form your client answers when they book — short answers, long answers, a choice, or a tick to confirm. For a lot of this work these are screening questions rather than paperwork: a heart condition, a pregnancy, whether someone has done this before, a confirmation that the session is not medical treatment.

Questions you mark as required are genuinely required — a client cannot book past one. They can revise their answers up until the session starts, and anyone who has not filled it in is nudged in their reminder email.

The questions themselves are visible on your profile. Someone deciding whether to book is entitled to see what they will be asked. Only the answers are private.

Chapter Four

Your hours

You set a recurring weekly grid — "Mondays 9–12 and 2–6" is two windows, and the gap is your lunch. Times are in your own timezone; clients see them converted to theirs.

On top of that sit four rules you set per service: the gap you want after each session, the minimum notice before someone can book, how far ahead they may book, and a daily cap. Add time off for a holiday, and existing bookings, and what a client is actually offered is the result of all of it.

Four interacting rules is easy to get wrong in a way that is completely silent. Twelve hours' notice, a two-hour buffer and a cap of one session a day can empty your calendar entirely, and the only symptom is that bookings stop arriving.

So the dashboard shows you the answer.

Chapter Five

Going live

When your profile is ready, Hilom publishes you. You appear in the directory, your status turns to Live, and clients can book.

A paid booking holds the slot while the client pays, and confirms the moment the payment lands. If they abandon checkout the hold lapses in about twenty minutes and the time frees itself. A free intro call confirms immediately. A package is paid for once, and each session inside it is booked afterwards.

Two people clicking the last remaining slot cannot both win it. That is guaranteed underneath the booking system rather than hoped for.

You and your client both get a confirmation, and a reminder about a day before.

Every time you are shown is in both zones. Yours first, your client's beside it. Their copy leads with theirs and shows yours. A Manila practitioner with a Sydney client is the ordinary case here, and one side doing the arithmetic in their head is how sessions get missed.

Chapter Six

Running sessions

Your Bookings tab is the day-to-day. Each session shows the time in both zones, who it is with, what they told you, and what you earn.

When you need to move a session

Cancelling refunds your client in full, whatever the notice — and for "something came up, could we do Thursday?" that is almost never what you want. So you can suggest a different time instead. You pick from your own free slots, add a note, and your client accepts or declines.

Nothing moves until they accept. The session stays exactly where it is while you wait, and you can withdraw the offer. There is no notice requirement on asking — needing to move tomorrow morning is exactly the case this exists for. What protects your client is that they can simply say no.

Talking to a client

Every booking has a thread. Messages reach your client by email with the text in them, so nobody has to come to the site to read one sentence, and a run of messages within a few minutes arrives as one email rather than three.

Booking someone in yourself

Not everything comes through the public flow. Someone pays you by bank transfer, you offer a session pro bono, you rebook a client as a goodwill gesture, or a long-standing client simply texts you. You can enter those directly: pick a service, type their email, choose a time. They do not need a Hilom account.

No money goes through Hilom for those, and Hilom owes you nothing for them. You have already been paid, or chosen not to be. There is a box to record what you were paid — it is a note for your own books, kept deliberately out of your Hilom earnings and payouts. Do not wait on a transfer for these.

Chapter Seven

Knowing your clients

A calendar answers "what is happening on Thursday". It cannot answer "who is this, and what have we done" — which is the question you have thirty seconds before a session with someone you last saw six weeks ago.

The Clients tab holds everyone you have seen, and for each person: every session, their intake answers, what they wrote when booking, and two kinds of note you write yourself.

  • About this client — the standing picture. What they are working on, what to remember, how you like to begin. You read it before every session and edit it constantly.
  • Notes on this session — what happened in one hour. Written afterwards, and belonging to that hour for good.

They are separate on purpose. One box for both would make every update to the first an edit to the history of the second.

Neither is ever shown to the client, or included in any email. Two practitioners seeing the same person keep entirely separate records. If you are unsure whether something is private you will write nothing useful — so, plainly: these are yours.

Chapter Eight

Reviews

After a paid session, your client is asked once — by email and on their bookings page — how it went. Only someone who booked and had a session can review, one review per session. There is no route for a review from a stranger.

Hilom reads every review before it appears. That is a check for abuse, contact details, and things a client may not have meant to make public. It is not a filter on unflattering opinions: a rating with the bad reviews removed tells a prospective client nothing, and would not be worth showing.

Reviews appear with the rating, the comment and the client's first name and last initial. Your average and review count show on your profile and directory card.

One thing to know before it happens: a client you marked as a no-show can still review you. They were charged and they had an experience. A rating that only counted the sessions that went well would measure the sessions rather than the practice, and would be worth less to everyone. If a review is unfair rather than merely unflattering, tell us — moderation happens before anything appears.

Chapter Nine

Getting paid

Hilom collects the payment and transfers your share. Your Earnings tab shows the split in full — never a single net number with the fee hidden inside it.

A session becomes payable once it has happened. A no-show still pays you: you held the time. A cancellation does not.

Packages pay as you deliver

A six-session package is one payment from your client, but you earn each session's share as that session happens — not all of it on purchase. If a client stops after two sessions, the remaining four were never paid out and nothing has to be clawed back from you.

For the same reason, cancelling one session of a package returns a credit to the client rather than money. They bought six sessions and they still have six.

You will get an email when a payout is sent, with the reference. You do not have to notice a number changing on a screen. Refunds and payouts are moved by a person at Hilom rather than automatically — recorded here, sent from the bank.

Chapter Ten

Fitting into your life

Video, in your own account

Connect your Google or Zoom account and Hilom creates a real meeting for each booking in your account, with you as host. Hilom holds no meeting account of its own. You can also just paste a standing room link if you prefer.

Your sessions in your own calendar

Subscribe to a private link in Google Calendar, Apple Calendar or Outlook and your Hilom sessions appear alongside the rest of your life, updating on their own. It is read-only: nothing you do in your calendar changes a booking.

Treat that link like a password. Anyone holding it can see your schedule — that is how every subscribable calendar works, because calendar apps cannot sign in. If you share it by accident, generate a new one and the old link stops working immediately. It is not created until you ask for one.

Going away

Set an "away until" date and new bookings pause without you having to delete your weekly hours and retype them later. Sessions already booked inside that window stay where they are — Hilom lists them for you rather than cancelling them on your behalf, because cancelling would refund every one of those clients in full off the back of a date field.

Worth knowing

The things people are surprised by

Better here than in the middle of a working week.

Approved is not listed. Hilom publishes you once your profile is ready, and you set that profile up in private first.

Sign in again after approval. Access attaches to a fresh sign-in, so an old session will keep telling you there is no dashboard.

Your refund thresholds are the policy. Existing services were left on 24 hours and 12 hours rather than having something guessed for them.

When you cancel, the client is always refunded in full — your own thresholds govern them changing their mind, not you changing yours.

A no-show pays you. A cancellation does not. You held the time either way, but only one of them was your client's choice on the day.

Package money arrives session by session. Selling a six-session block does not pay you six sessions' worth this month.

Sessions you book in yourself pay you nothing through Hilom, by design — Hilom never collected for them.

Suggesting a new time changes nothing until the client accepts, and the slot is not held while you wait.

Empty availability means no bookings, deliberately. If you cannot work out why your calendar is quiet, the availability preview will tell you.

Your client notes are never shown to the client.

Unflattering reviews are published. Moderation is for abuse and privacy, not for protecting an average.
